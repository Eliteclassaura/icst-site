# Cooling tower parts

## What we supply

ICS supplies replacement parts for field-erected cooling towers across the common makes and configurations: wet, hybrid or dry, counterflow or crossflow, induced or forced draft.

The quality of a part decides how the whole tower performs, so parts are quoted against your tower and its duty rather than ordered from a list. Send the make, model, cell arrangement and duty, along with any documentation you hold for the tower, and the enquiry is scoped against it.

## Part families

### Fans and fan stacks

Axial fans in fibreglass and aluminium blade for cooling towers, air-cooled condensers and heat exchangers, in diameters from a few feet to the largest field-erected cells. FRP fan stacks for counterflow or crossflow towers are custom designed to ICS specifications, in the diameter and height the cell requires.

A fan stack is a structural and aerodynamic component, not a cover. A belled inlet reduces inlet losses, and the conical diverging section above the fan converts velocity pressure back into static pressure, and stack sections joined by lap joint or butt flange allow the stack diameter and the blade tip clearance to be adjusted in the field.

### Drive shafts and couplings

Composite and elastomeric drive shafts and coupling assemblies, including replacements compatible with the Addax and Amarillo LRA, LRF, LRR and LRX series. Manufacturer names and model designations are used to identify what a part replaces; all marks are the property of their owners. A coupling has to transmit torque, absorb vibration and tolerate misalignment between the gearbox and the fan; composite shafts are lighter and corrosion resistant, and maintenance on most designs needs only minimal shaft movement or an element replacement without disturbing the machinery either side.

### Gearboxes and gear drives

Right angle, parallel and vertical gear drives in single and double reduction, in all sizes and ratios. Special order gears and parts are welcome.

### Motors

Motors for cooling tower duty, for standard and variable-speed operation.

### Fill media

Film, splash and hybrid fill, for counterflow and crossflow towers.

Fill is the medium that creates the surface area where heat transfer happens. Hot water flowing through it either breaks into droplets or spreads into a thin film, depending on the fill type, which increases contact with the air and drives evaporation.

**Splash fill** uses horizontal slats and bars. Water striking them breaks into droplets. Its open design tolerates debris and poor water quality, resists clogging, and is easier to clean than film, but it needs more height, more fan power and more structural support. Modern splash fill is PVC or polypropylene rather than the wood used originally.

**Film fill** is closely packed PVC sheet, flat or textured and folded, which spreads water into a thin film over a large surface area in a compact volume. For a given volume it transfers more heat than splash fill, but impurities, debris, solids, bacteria, oils and fats build up in it and cost performance, so it suits clean process water with proper filtration and a working treatment programme.

**Hybrid fill** is supplied in packs like film fill but with large flutes and minimal or no cross-fluting. It is the choice where film-fill performance is wanted and the water is not clean enough for standard film fill, because it tolerates debris, solids, oils, fats and other fouling agents.

Material follows hot-water temperature. PVC is the usual film fill material up to its continuous service limit; above that limit polypropylene is specified. The limit is a property of the fill supplied, so the figure to design against is the one on that fill's data sheet, not a rule of thumb.

### Drift eliminators

Cellular and blade drift eliminators for counterflow and crossflow towers.

Drift is water carried out of the tower in the exit airstream, and it is usually a permit condition before it is anything else: the allowable drift rate is written into the tower's specification as a percentage of circulating water flow. It also has mechanical and water-balance consequences. Droplets propelled at velocity through the plenum erode fan blades, drive shafts, gearboxes and the mechanical supports around them, and drift adds to make-up water demand. A cellular eliminator forces the air through a succession of direction changes so that centrifugal force separates the droplets out of the stream and drains them back to the wet section.

### Nozzles

Counterflow and crossflow nozzles, including non-clog and quick-change orifice designs.

### FRP structural members

FRP angles, beams, channels, deck board, flat sheet and square tube.

### Vibration switches and monitoring

Vibration switches, transmitters, accelerometers and machinery monitoring systems for cooling tower mechanical equipment.

### Tower hardware

Stainless steel, galvanised and FRP connectors, anchors, fasteners and structural fittings, for FRP, steel and hybrid tower structures. These components sit in a wet, chemically treated and corrosive environment, so the material choice matters more than the part number.
